1. Product Overview
The Integrated POS, Inventory & Business Management System is a centralized retail platform designed to manage point-of-sale operations, inventory, customers, suppliers, purchasing, cash registers, online orders, quotations, promotions, budgets, accounts payable, and financial reporting from a single system.
The platform is intended to support individual stores as well as future multi-store and multi-terminal deployments. Real-time synchronization between sales, inventory, online orders, and business reporting is a core design objective.
2. Problem Statement
Many small and medium-sized businesses rely on separate systems or manual processes for sales, inventory, accounting, suppliers, and online orders. This creates operational inefficiencies and makes it difficult to maintain accurate business records.
Incorrect or delayed inventory quantities.
Duplicate or inconsistent data entry.
Limited visibility into sales performance.
Difficult supplier and payable management.
Cash-register discrepancies.
Manual end-of-day calculations.
Poor tracking of expired or near-expiration products.
Limited financial and management reporting.
Difficulty coordinating physical and online sales channels.
3. Product Goals
Provide a fast, reliable, and user-friendly POS experience.
Maintain accurate and near-real-time inventory quantities.
Support multiple POS terminals and registers.
Centralize customer and supplier management.
Track accounts payable and supplier payments.
Provide controlled cash-register opening and closing workflows.
Support online orders using the same inventory system as physical POS sales.
Provide quotations, invoices, receipts, and printable labels.
Support flexible discounts and promotional rules.
Track product batches and expiration dates where applicable.
Provide operational and financial reporting.
Implement role-based access control and audit logging.
Provide a scalable foundation for multi-store expansion.

6. POS & Sales Management
The POS module is the core transactional component of the system.
Start and manage POS sessions.
Select a register and terminal.
Search products by name, SKU, or barcode.
Scan product barcodes.
Add, remove, and modify cart items.
Apply authorized discounts and promotions.
Associate sales with customers.
Calculate taxes and totals.
Accept multiple payment methods.
Support suspended/held transactions and later resumption.
Complete sales and generate receipts.
Reprint receipts where authorized.
Process returns, refunds, and exchanges.
Illustrative POS workflow:
Cashier Login → Select Register → Scan/Search Products → Cart → Discount/Promotion → Tax → Payment → Complete Sale → Receipt → Inventory Update
7. Inventory & Stock Management
The inventory engine shall maintain a reliable transaction history for every stock movement.
Stock receiving.
Stock adjustment.
Stock transfer between locations/registers where applicable.
Stock deduction from completed sales.
Stock returns.
Damaged and expired stock processing.
Inventory reconciliation.
Stock movement history.
Inventory valuation.
Minimum and maximum stock thresholds.
Illustrative stock calculation:
Opening Stock      100
Purchase           +50
POS Sale           -20
Online Sale         -5
Damaged             -2
Adjustment          +3
----------------------
Current Stock      126
8. Expiration Date & Stock Alerts
Products that require expiry management may be tracked by batch/lot, manufacturing date, and expiration date.
Identify expired products.
Identify products expiring today.
Identify products expiring within configurable periods such as 7 or 30 days.
Generate inventory alerts.
Prevent or warn against sale of expired stock according to business rules.
Support batch-level inventory tracking.
Low-stock alerts shall be generated when current stock reaches or falls below the configured minimum stock level.

12. Cash Register & Day-End Closing
Each physical register shall operate through an independent cash session.
Opening workflow:
Cashier → Select Register → Enter Opening Cash → Start Session
Closing workflow:
Close Register → Calculate Expected Cash → Enter Actual Cash → Calculate Variance → Generate Z Report → Close Session
The day-end report should include:
Register and terminal.
Cashier.
Opening cash.
Cash sales.
Card and other payment totals.
Refunds.
Discounts.
Cash-in and cash-out transactions.
Expected closing cash.
Actual closing cash.
Cash variance.
Transaction count.
Opening and closing timestamps.
Example reconciliation:
Opening Cash        $200
Cash Sales        $1,500
Cash Refunds        -$50
Cash Added          +$100
Cash Removed         -$20
-------------------------
Expected Cash      $1,730
Actual Cash        $1,720
Variance             -$10
13. Multiple Service Terminals
The system shall support multiple service terminals/registers operating concurrently within a store while maintaining separate sessions and complete transaction attribution.
Store #1
  ├── Register 01 → Cashier A
  ├── Register 02 → Cashier B
  └── Register 03 → Cashier C
Each transaction should identify the store, terminal, register, cashier, and register session.
